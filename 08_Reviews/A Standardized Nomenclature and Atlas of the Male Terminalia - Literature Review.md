# Literature Review — A Standardized Nomenclature and Atlas of the Male Terminalia of Drosophila melanogaster

> **Source:** [[A Standardized Nomenclature and Atlas of the Male Terminalia of Drosophila melanogaster]] (Rice, David, Kamimura, Masly, McGregor, Nagy, Noselli, Nunes, O'Grady, Sánchez-Herrero, Siegal, Toda, Rebeiz, Courtier-Orgogozo & Yassin, *Fly*, 2019)
> **DOI:** [10.1080/19336934.2019.1653733](https://doi.org/10.1080/19336934.2019.1653733)

---

## 1. Background & Significance

The male terminalia of *D. melanogaster* are a powerful model for studying genital evolution, but the field was hampered by **inconsistent terminology** — the same structures called different names across papers. This paper provides a **standardized nomenclature and visual atlas** of the male terminalia, including a list of synonymous terms, to unify the community.

## 2. Purpose

- Provide a **detailed visual anatomy** of male genital parts.
- Establish a **standardized nomenclature** with a list of **synonymous terms**.
- Serve as a **reference atlas** for the community (complemented by the female atlas, McQueen et al., 2022).

## 3. Key Content

- **Visual atlas** of male terminalia structures (epandrium, posterior lobe, clasper, phallus/aedeagus, hypandrium, anal plate, etc.).
- **Standardized names** for each structure, with synonyms.
- A consensus from **many labs** (15 authors) to harmonize terminology.

The atlas presents dissected cuticular structures paired with color-coded line diagrams, decomposing the terminalia into discrete named sclerites, with standardized orientation and consistent color coding across views. **Interpretation:** the atlas standardizes male terminalia anatomy by linking real dissected morphology to idealized color-coded outlines, enabling consistent cross-study comparison — the foundation for the developmental atlases that followed.

## 5. Discussion & Implications

- **Standardized terminology** is essential for comparative and developmental work on genitalia.
- This atlas underpins the molecular atlases ([[An Atlas of Transcription Factors Expressed in Male Pupal Terminalia of Drosophila melanogaster]], [[Parallels in the regulatory landscape of dimorphic female and male genital structures in Drosophila melanogaster]]) and the cross-species atlas ([[A developmental atlas of male terminalia across twelve species of Drosophila]]).

## 6. Limitations

- A **reference/nomenclature** paper rather than a functional study.
- Focuses on **male** terminalia; the female counterpart is McQueen et al. (2022).
