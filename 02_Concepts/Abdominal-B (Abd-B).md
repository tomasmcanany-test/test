# Abdominal-B (Abd-B)

## Definition

**Abdominal-B (Abd-B)** is a **Hox gene** in the **Bithorax complex (BX-C)** of
*Drosophila melanogaster* that specifies the identity of the posterior
abdominal segments, including the segments that give rise to the **terminalia**.

## Key Facts

- Abd-B is the most posterior Hox gene in the BX-C.
- It has two major protein isoforms with distinct functions: **Abd-B m**
  (morphogenetic) and **Abd-B r** (regulatory), produced by alternative
  promoters and splicing.
- Abd-B is required for the correct development of the posterior abdomen and
  the **genital disc**.
- It acts together with the sex-determination pathway (**doublesex**) to
  pattern the terminalia.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- Abd-B encodes a homeodomain transcription factor.
- Its expression is graded along the posterior abdomen, with the highest levels
  in the most posterior segments.
- Abd-B regulates downstream targets that execute posterior segment and genital
  development.

## Development & Function

- Establishes posterior identity of the terminalia-forming segments and the
  genital disc.

## Evolution

- Abd-B and its regulation are conserved across insects; changes in its
  expression/function contribute to diversification of posterior morphology.
