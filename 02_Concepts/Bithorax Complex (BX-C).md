# Bithorax Complex (BX-C)

## Definition

The **Bithorax complex (BX-C)** is one of the two **Hox** gene clusters in
*Drosophila melanogaster*. It contains the genes that specify the identity of
the posterior thoracic and abdominal segments, including **Abd-B**, which
patterns the segments that give rise to the **terminalia**.

## Key Facts

- BX-C comprises three homeotic genes: **Ultrabithorax (Ubx)**,
  **abdominal-A (abd-A)**, and **Abdominal-B (Abd-B)**.
- The other Hox cluster is the **Antennapedia complex (ANT-C)**.
- BX-C genes are expressed in nested domains along the posterior body axis.
- **Abd-B** is the most posterior BX-C gene and is essential for
  **terminalia**/genital development.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- BX-C is regulated by cis-regulatory elements and chromatin regulators
  (Polycomb/Trithorax) that maintain stable segment-specific expression.

## Development & Function

- Assigns segmental identity to the posterior thorax and abdomen.

## Evolution

- BX-C organization and function are conserved across insects; the complex is a
  paradigm for how clustered Hox genes pattern the body axis.
