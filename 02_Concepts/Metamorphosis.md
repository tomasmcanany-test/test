# Metamorphosis

## Definition

**Metamorphosis** is the developmental transition in holometabolous insects
(such as *Drosophila*) in which the larva transforms into the adult. During
this process, larval tissues are remodeled and **imaginal discs** evert and
differentiate into adult structures, including the **terminalia**.

## Key Facts

- Triggered by the steroid hormone **ecdysone** and the sesquiterpenoid
  **juvenile hormone**.
- **Imaginal discs** proliferate in the larva, then evert, spread, and
  differentiate into adult cuticle at metamorphosis.
- The **genital disc** gives rise to the adult **terminalia** during this
  transition.
- Pupa formation and adult eclosion are key landmarks.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- Ecdysone receptor (EcR) and its isoforms mediate the hormone response.
- Pulses of ecdysone drive the larval–pupal and pupal–adult transitions.

## Development & Function

- Disc eversion and differentiation produce the adult exoskeleton, including
  genital and anal structures.

## Evolution

- Holometaboly (complete metamorphosis) is a major innovation in insect
  evolution; the redeployment of imaginal discs underlies much adult
  morphological diversity.
