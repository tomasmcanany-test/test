# Hox Genes

## Definition

**Hox genes** are a family of conserved homeobox-containing transcription
factors that specify **segmental identity** along the anterior–posterior body
axis. In *Drosophila melanogaster* they are organized into two gene complexes:
the **Antennapedia complex (ANT-C)** and the **Bithorax complex (BX-C)**.

## Key Facts

- Hox genes contain a conserved **homeobox** encoding a DNA-binding
  homeodomain.
- They are expressed in overlapping domains along the body axis and assign each
  segment its characteristic morphology.
- In the posterior abdomen, **Abd-B** (in the BX-C) is the Hox gene that
  patterns the segments that give rise to the **terminalia**.
- Hox genes are **evolutionarily conserved** across animals and are central to
  both development and **evo-devo**.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- Hox proteins are transcription factors that regulate batteries of
  downstream target genes.
- Their expression is maintained by Polycomb/Trithorax group chromatin
  regulators.
- Colinearity: order of genes in the complex correlates with order of
  expression domains along the axis.

## Development & Function

- Hox input establishes the posterior identity of the **genital disc** and
  terminalia-forming segments.

## Evolution

- Hox clusters are ancient and conserved; changes in Hox expression and
  targets underlie morphological divergence across arthropods and animals.
