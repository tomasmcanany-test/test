# Morphogen Signaling

## Definition

A **morphogen** is a signaling molecule that forms a concentration gradient
across a field of cells and provides **positional information**, causing
different cells to adopt different fates depending on the local concentration.
Morphogen signaling is central to patterning the **imaginal discs**, including
the **genital disc**.

## Key Facts

- Key morphogen pathways in *Drosophila* discs include **Wnt/Wingless**,
  **Hedgehog (Hh)**, and **Dpp/BMP** (Decapentaplegic).
- Gradients of these signals subdivide discs into domains with distinct fates.
- In the **genital disc**, morphogen signaling cooperates with **Hox** input
  (**Abd-B**) and **doublesex** to pattern the **terminalia**.
- Morphogen interpretation involves signal transduction cascades and
  downstream transcription factors (e.g., Cubitus interruptus for Hh, Mad for
  Dpp).

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- **Wingless/Wnt**: Frizzled receptors → Armadillo/β-catenin.
- **Hedgehog**: Patched/Smoothened → Cubitus interruptus (Ci).
- **Dpp/BMP**: Thickveins receptors → Mad/Medea (SMADs).

## Development & Function

- Establish compartment boundaries and graded positional information within
  discs during larval development.

## Evolution

- Morphogen pathways are highly conserved; changes in their deployment
  underlie morphological divergence (a core theme of **evo-devo**).
