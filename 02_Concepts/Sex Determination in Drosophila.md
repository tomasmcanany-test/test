# Sex Determination in Drosophila

## Definition

In *Drosophila melanogaster*, **sex is determined by the ratio of X chromosomes
to autosomes (X:A ratio)**, which sets off a cascade of alternative splicing
that culminates in sex-specific transcription factors. This pathway controls
all sexually dimorphic traits, including the **terminalia/genitalia**.

## Key Facts

- The **X:A ratio** controls the primary signal gene **Sex-lethal (Sxl)**.
- **Sxl → transformer (tra) / transformer-2 (tra-2) → doublesex (dsx)** is the
  core regulatory cascade.
- Sex-specific splicing of *dsx* produces **dsxF** (female) or **dsxM** (male)
  isoforms.
- *fruitless (fru)* also acts downstream of tra and controls male courtship
  behavior and some male-specific neural/structural features.
- The terminalia are among the most prominent sexually dimorphic outputs of this
  pathway.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- Sxl is only active in females (XX); it regulates splicing of *tra*.
- Active Tra (with Tra-2) drives female-specific splicing of *dsx* and *fru*.
- In males (XY), Tra is inactive and default (male) splicing occurs.

## Development & Function

- The pathway acts in the **genital disc** to impose sex-specific identity on
  shared primordia, producing male vs. female **terminalia**.

## Evolution

- Although the upstream signal differs across insects, **dsx** is a conserved
  terminal switch, illustrating how the downstream logic of sex determination
  is evolutionarily conserved.
