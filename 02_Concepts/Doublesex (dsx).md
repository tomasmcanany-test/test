# Doublesex (dsx)

## Definition

**Doublesex (dsx)** is a transcription factor in *Drosophila melanogaster* that
acts as the terminal switch in the **sex-determination** pathway. It is
sex-specifically spliced to produce a **female (dsxF)** or **male (dsxM)**
isoform, which direct sexually dimorphic development — including the
**terminalia/genitalia**.

## Key Facts

- The *dsx* gene is alternatively spliced in a sex-specific manner downstream of
  the **Sxl → tra/tra-2 → dsx** cascade.
- **dsxF** and **dsxM** share a common DNA-binding domain but differ in their
  C-terminal domains, giving them distinct regulatory activities.
- dsx controls the sexually dimorphic development of the ** genital disc** and
  adult **terminalia**.
- A related gene, *fruitless (fru)*, also acts downstream of tra and controls
  aspects of male behavior and some male-specific structures.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- dsx is a member of the conserved **DM domain** family of transcription
  factors, present across animals (including vertebrates, where it regulates
  sexual differentiation).
- Its targets include genes that pattern the genital disc in a sex-specific way.

## Development & Function

- In the genital disc, dsx imposes male vs. female identity on shared
  primordia, generating the sexually dimorphic adult terminalia.

## Evolution

- The DM-domain/Doublesex/MAB-3 family is conserved from nematodes to
  vertebrates, making dsx a key molecule for understanding the **evolution of
  sexual dimorphism** and genitalia.
