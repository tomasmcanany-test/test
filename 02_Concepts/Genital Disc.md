# Genital Disc

## Definition

The **genital disc** is the larval imaginal disc that gives rise to the adult
**terminalia** (external genitalia and analia) of *Drosophila melanogaster*. It
is a single, bilaterally symmetric epithelial primordium formed by the fusion
of genital and anal primordia from the posterior abdominal segments.

## Key Facts

- Unlike the wing or leg discs, the genital disc is **not strictly segmental**;
  it is a composite structure derived from multiple posterior segments.
- It is **sexually dimorphic** at the level of gene expression, and its adult
  derivatives differ markedly between males and females.
- The disc is patterned by **Hox** input (**Abd-B**) and the sex-determination
  effector **doublesex (dsx)**.
- During **metamorphosis** the disc everts and differentiates into the adult
  terminalia.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- **Abd-B** establishes posterior identity of the disc.
- **dsx** (male/female isoforms) directs sexually dimorphic patterning.
- Local signaling (Wingless/Wnt, Hedgehog, Dpp/BMP) and transcription factors
  subdivide the disc into genital and anal domains.

## Development & Function

- In larvae the disc proliferates as an epithelial sac; its cells are
  determined to form specific adult cuticular structures.
- Compartmentalization into genital vs. anal primordia is a key early step.

## Evolution

- The genital disc is a favored model for studying how a shared primordium
  generates divergent, rapidly evolving adult morphologies across species.
