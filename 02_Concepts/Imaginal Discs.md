# Imaginal Discs

## Definition

**Imaginal discs** are epithelial sacs in the *Drosophila* larva that give
rise to the adult cuticular structures (wings, legs, eyes, genitalia, etc.)
during **metamorphosis**. Each disc is a determined primordium whose cells
proliferate in the larva and then differentiate into adult structures.

## Key Facts

- There are distinct discs for the eyes/antennae, wings, halteres, legs, and
  **genitalia**.
- Disc cells are **determined** (committed to a fate) early in development even
  though they do not differentiate until metamorphosis.
- Discs are patterned by **morphogen gradients** (Wingless/Wnt, Hedgehog,
  Dpp/BMP) and by **Hox genes** that assign positional identity.
- The **genital disc** is the disc that produces the **terminalia**.

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- Compartment boundaries and signaling centers (e.g., the anterior/posterior
  boundary) organize patterning within each disc.
- **Hox genes** and selector genes assign disc identity along the body axis.

## Development & Function

- Larval growth → eversion and differentiation at metamorphosis → adult
  cuticular structure.

## Evolution

- Because discs are discrete, tractable primordia, they are a central model
  in **evo-devo** for how conserved signaling generates diverse morphology.
