# Evolution of Genitalia

## Definition

The **evolution of genitalia** refers to the observation that external genital
structures are among the **most rapidly evolving and morphologically divergent**
traits in animals, especially insects. In *Drosophila*, male and female
terminalia differ markedly between closely related species and are often used
for species identification.

## Key Facts

- Genital morphology evolves **faster** than most other body parts (a pattern
  termed "rapid divergent evolution" of genitalia).
- Hypotheses for this rapid evolution include **sexual selection** (e.g.,
  cryptic female choice, male–male competition) and **reinforcement** /
  reproductive isolation (lock-and-key).
- Despite divergent outcomes, the underlying **developmental toolkit** is conserved (Hox genes,
  **doublesex**, signaling pathways).

## Molecular / Genetic Basis

- **dsx** and **Abd-B** pattern the genital primordium; changes in their
  regulation and targets underlie interspecific differences.
- Cis-regulatory changes are thought to be a major driver of genitalia
  divergence.

## Development & Function

- The **genital disc** is a shared primordium whose developmental program is
  tweaked to produce species-specific morphology.

## Evolution

- Genitalia are a premier model for linking **evo-devo**, **sexual selection**,
  and **speciation**.
